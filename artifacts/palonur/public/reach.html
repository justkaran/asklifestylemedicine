The shift that is happening nowThe gap

The gap we are closing

150 million lifestyle health questions are asked via AI every day sleep, nutrition, stress, meaningfulness, gratitude. This user behavior is forming now, and not one of those answers is in partnership with a trusted institution. The default health advisor for a generation is currently being formed in real time.

The user

Gets medically and scientifically unvetted advice, delivered with full AI confidence. Take this user's case: Emma, 55 years old, can't tell wellness theater from trustworthy advice. She can't sleep waking up at 3 a.m., night after night. She asks a chatbot. Gets a confident, well-sourced answer. And has absolutely no idea whether to believe it. 

The institution

Stanford's research isn't credited in Emma's answer. By design. Neither is the guidance behind the 150 million similar questions people ask AI chatbots every day. The world's best health science sits behind the interface, never inside it. No one designed for Emma.

The citations exist. But the research is a moving target: a faculty member can revise her position on a paper she published in 2008. Palonur knows immediately. LLMs don't, their knowledge is always partial, frozen at the moment they were trained. If they want to fetch from updated data, they will need to refer to us. The trust architecture LLMs can't deliver, Palonur can.

Without it, AI in health is irrelevance disguised as progress.

The AI company

ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Grok try to manufacture institutional credibility by training across many institutions at once, which strips authorship and muddies the source. Palonur is not another LLM in that race. We are the credible source those systems route to. The fountain, not the river: each institution's content stays clean, attributed, intact. They can solve this only with us.

Same gap, three angles. The user, the university, and the AI company are all stuck on one missing piece, a credible, attributable answer. We are that piece.

We start where AI already owns the relationship: lifestyle medicine, beginning with Sleep, led by Prof. Jamie Zeitzer at Stanford. One pillar at a time, faculty in control. Live distribution from day one, Stanford Lifestyle Medicine's site already draws ~120k monthly visitors asking exactly these questions, with zero paid acquisition.

The Technology

How it works, and why it is defensible.

A question routes through a Stanford-curated corpus, is answered in a structured format and ships with a queryable receipt. The corpus, and the signed-answer ledger are the assets a competitor cannot scrape into existence and all is built, tested, and in production today.

The verification pipelineIn production

A question becomes a signed Stanford answer.

Seven stages plus a closed loop: science as the source of truth. Every stage runs on every consumer query in production today. There is no prompt-only fallback: every shipped answer has a human name attached, or it is refused, on the record.

1 · Question

Consumer app or partner API call. SSE streaming; partner calls pass through a tiered, metered API key.

2 · Retrieve

Pillar-keyed vector search over the Stanford-curated corpus. Faculty-approved interpretations weighted 1.25× over raw paper chunks.

3 · Structure

Model emits five labeled sections + two refusal modes. Structure enforced server-side, not requested in prose.

4 · Faculty queue if Necessary

Pillar steward reviews delicate questions, edits, approves. Only the named pillar steward's team can approve, enforced by middleware.

5 · Sign

Approval writes signer + timestamp + version + content hash. The signer is queryable from any answer ever shipped.

6 · Serve & Protect

Cached, attributed answer returned. Rate-limit + concurrency caps, ToS headers attached. Source never leaves our servers.

7 · Receipt

A SQL-queryable graph: question → corpus chunk → paper → faculty signer → timestamp. The receipt is the product; the chat is the demo.

∞ · Closed loop

Every uncovered query is logged, clustered, and surfaced to the right pillar's inbox, end-to-end tested.

Refuse path

If the answer isn't in the faculty corpus, we return REFUSE / UNCOVERED. No silent confabulation. Refusal compliance is a first-class numeric metric, measured on every release via a blinded faculty-graded eval harness with adversarial prompt-injection seeds in the test set.

Three things a competitor cannot reproduceDefensibility

A new entrant can copy stationary RAG. They cannot copy this.

The structural barrier. LLMs are built to be the source of truth, they cannot defer to an external authority and remain themselves. Universities will not hand their breathing research to a model that strips authorship. The control point sits exactly where OpenAI and co structurally cannot go.

1 · The receipt graph exists in production today

Every served answer joins back to the approving faculty member and the underlying paper, in SQL, with timestamps. "Zeitzer approved this on 2026-04-12" is queryable from any answer ID, not a marketing claim, an audit log. Cryptographic per-answer signing ships when the first partner contract requires it.


2 · The refusal path is a measured first-class metric

REFUSE and UNCOVERED are enforced output tokens. A blinded faculty-graded eval harness produces a numeric refusal-compliance rate on every release, against a seed set that includes deliberate adversarial prompt injections.

Its Stanford's answer, or no answer.

3 · The faculty queue + governed retriever change outputs mechanically

Faculty-approved interpretations are weighted 1.25× in retrieval, so steward work materially shifts what the model says. Every uncovered query is logged, clustered, and surfaced to the right pillar's inbox, end-to-end tested.

We have a user insight: Faculty want to curate their papers. A large part that drives the moat is in the curation, encoded. A competitor must build the bench, the queue, the lifecycle, the clustering, and the eval discipline with the relationships first. 

The loop: more faculty → more credible answers → more query demand → more visibility → more faculty. Mass attracts mass. It compounds, and it is already compounding.

Section 2 of 3Onboarding

Onboarding

Three sides, one loop.

Faculty supply credibility. AI providers supply distribution. Users supply demand. Each side has a distinct, low-friction way in and each turn of the loop lowers our cost to add the next faculty member.

Three doors, one engineSign-up

How and why each side comes on.

Faculty

Our first step is onboarding all seven pillars of the Stanford Lifestyle Medicine Program (SLM) as Palonurs. The system will generate a base RAG from existing content, and each pillar steward's team will be able to review answers through an AI-supported queue. The warm introduction comes naturally through the existing SLM structure at almost no CAC.

Because answers are cached, faculty time scales with the log of usage not linearly. The more Palonur is used, the less it costs them per answer. In return, they receive authorship credit and lab visibility at scale, across every AI platform their content appears on.

Low time commitment and broad visibility are the core reasons faculty will want to participate. We will also reimburse each contributor: every time a faculty member's interpretation or content is served in response to a query, they are compensated for that call. The exact pricing model is still to be determined.

End users

The low-hanging fruit is already there: the Stanford Lifestyle Medicine site draws roughly 120,000 active users a month with 390.000 "events". Day one, zero paid acquisition.

We are offering a consumer surface that acts as a personal OS for longevity, starting with sleep, then expanding across the other SLM pillars: nutrition, stress, gratitude, cognitive enhancement, social engagement, and physical fitness. It combines tracking and structured courses into a coherent companion that understands how these pillars interact, how sleep shapes nutrition, how nutrition shapes sleep, rather than treating each one in isolation.

AI providers

Self-serve. One documented endpoint and a machine-readable spec, keyed, metered, ToS prohibits training, receipts prove which partner served which answer when. Developer integration converts to an enterprise contract as volume grows.

Faculty supply credibility, AI providers supply distribution, users supply demand and each turn of the loop lowers our cost to add the next faculty member.

Why the Margin Expands

Approved answers are cached by question cluster and corpus version. When a similar question comes in, it is served from cache, no faculty involvement required. Only genuinely novel or sensitive questions escalate to the faculty queue. We estimate this will represent fewer than 1% of all queries, a figure we will need to validate.

The implication is structural: faculty time grows with the logarithm of usage, not linearly. Doubling the user base does not double their workload. Each additional partner therefore adds volume with minimal added cost, approaching pure margin.
