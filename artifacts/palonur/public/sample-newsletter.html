This month's feature · Sleep

Three ways to reset your circadian clock

By the Stanford Lifestyle Medicine faculty

Your body runs on an internal 24-hour clock that decides when you feel alert and when you feel sleepy. When that clock drifts out of sync with your schedule, you feel it as restless nights and groggy mornings. The good news: the same clock that drifts can be nudged back — gently, and without medication.

First, anchor your morning. Getting bright light within an hour of waking is the strongest signal you can send your clock that the day has begun. Second, keep your wake time steady, even on weekends — a consistent morning does more for your sleep than a consistent bedtime. Third, dim the evening: lower the lights and step away from screens an hour before bed, so melatonin can rise on its own.

"A consistent morning does more for your sleep than a perfect bedtime."

None of these requires a new gadget or a strict regimen — just a few small, repeatable choices. Pick one this week, and let the others follow.
