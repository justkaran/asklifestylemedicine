Palonur Confidential · For investors
Business plan · Stanford Lifestyle Medicine newsletter & subscription

A trusted health newsletter, delivered through Palonur — built on the Harvard Health model, with an AI layer Harvard never had.

Stanford Lifestyle Medicine (SLM) reaches roughly 120,000 visitors a month with no paid acquisition. This plan turns that audience into a recurring subscription — a curated newsletter plus a faculty-grounded AI agent that answers subscribers' lifestyle-health questions. Palonur builds and operates the product; investors cover a lean monthly cost base up front and are recovered out of subscription revenue. What this plan sets out to do
  • Turn an existing audience into a subscription. Convert SLM's ~120k monthly visitors into recurring members with a curated newsletter and a faculty-grounded AI agent — with no paid acquisition.
  • Recover a lean cost base, then hand the majority to Stanford. Investors fund a flat operating cost; once it is repaid, the revenue split steps decisively in SLM's favor.
  • Give back as a year-end gift that builds the Institute. SLM's share is accrued and gifted annually — funding a future Stanford Lifestyle Medicine Institute, a dedicated post-doc, and Amy's leadership.
  • Credit and reimburse the faculty. The experts whose science powers the answers are recognised and reimbursed from a defined share of revenue.

The opportunity

Harvard Health Publishing turned trusted medical content into a durable consumer-subscription business. SLM has the same raw ingredient — credible Stanford lifestyle-medicine science — plus a distribution channel already running and an audience already asking health questions. What was missing is the product and the operating partner. Palonur is both.

The product

A subscription that pairs an editor-curated newsletter with a faculty-grounded AI agent. Every answer is sourced from Stanford-approved content and signed by a named expert — the same trust layer Palonur already runs in production for the sleep pillar.

The deal

Subscription income is shared with a structure that flips at investor recovery: Palonur takes a larger share (60%) while it repays the investor-funded cost base, then steps down to 30% once investors are whole — so SLM's share rises to 70% for the long term. The SLM portion is not an ongoing payout — it is accrued through the year and distributed at year-end as a single gift. Investors fund Palonur's ~$40k/month cost base (the post-doc and support for Amy) and are repaid out of the recovery-phase share.

Where the gift goes

There are only two destinations for subscription revenue: Palonur's share, which repays the investor-funded cost base, and SLM's year-end gift. That single gift is the engine behind the longer-term goal — a standing Stanford Lifestyle Medicine Institute, a dedicated post-doc, and operating support for Amy.

PalonurThe product · subscription model
Product & subscription model

A newsletter people read, an agent they can ask.

Adapted from the Harvard Health subscription playbook — a trusted institution monetising its own content directly — and extended with the thing Harvard could not offer: a live AI agent grounded only in faculty-approved science.

What a subscriber gets

  • The SLM newsletter. Editor-curated lifestyle-medicine briefings — sleep, nutrition, stress, movement — written from Stanford faculty content.
  • Tailored AI answers — and action. Ask a question and get an answer drawn only from approved Stanford sources and signed by a named expert — then a concrete next step: a protocol, a habit, a "try this for seven nights," not just a citation.
  • Myth-busting. Plain-language takedowns of the wellness misinformation subscribers meet every day, each grounded in faculty science and the paper behind it.
  • Courses. Short, faculty-backed lifestyle-medicine courses — sleep, nutrition, stress — that turn the science into a guided programme a member can actually follow.
  • Reader stories. Anonymised member experiences, already part of the Palonur Stories pipeline, feed the newsletter.
  • Archive & receipts. Every answer is attributable back to the paper and the approver — trust you can audit.

Why it converts

  • Warm audience. ~120k monthly visitors already arrive asking lifestyle-health questions, with zero paid acquisition.
  • Named credibility. Stanford faculty attribution is something no general AI assistant can offer.
  • Two reasons to pay. Passive reading (the newsletter) and active answers (the agent) — content plus utility in one price.
  • Built, not hypothetical. The agent, the newsletter tooling, and the stories pipeline are already in production.
The Palonur partnership

SLM owns the brand, the faculty relationships, and the audience. Palonur builds and runs the technology, the editorial tooling, billing, and the AI agent — and carries the operating cost. The split — Palonur-favored only until investors are recovered, then settling into a Stanford-favored steady state, with the SLM share gifted at year-end — keeps the overwhelming majority of long-term value with Stanford while giving Palonur a clear path to recover the investor-funded cost base. It is a partnership structured as a gift, not a vendor contract.

The two-part price (Harvard model + AI)

Harvard Health sells an annual subscription to its content. We keep that — an affordable annual plan anchored to the newsletter — and add a modest monthly AI-usage component for the agent. The exact figure is still to be confirmed; the price below is a clearly-labeled placeholder you can change in one place on the next page, and the whole model recomputes.

What investors fund

Investors cover Palonur's monthly cost base from launch. There is no marketing spend in the base case — distribution comes free through the SLM audience — so the cost is dominated by the people who build and steward the product: a post-doc on the science side and operating support for Amy, plus infrastructure.

Because the cost base is flat and lean, the payback math is simple: after a 3% payment processing fee, as subscribers compound, Palonur's recovery-phase share crosses the cost line, and from that month forward the business self-funds — at which point the split steps down in SLM's favor.

Why the SLM share is a gift

The SLM portion is deliberately structured as a gift rather than a vendor payment. It is accrued through the year and distributed at year-end as a single contribution to Stanford Lifestyle Medicine. This keeps the relationship philanthropic and mission-aligned, and keeps the overwhelming majority of subscription value with Stanford.

It also funds the longer-term goal: a standing Stanford Lifestyle Medicine Institute, a dedicated post-doc, and Amy's leadership — all paid for out of this single year-end gift.

The recovery curve

Cumulative subscribers (gold, right axis) compound off the visitor base while Palonur's cumulative cash position (left axis) runs negative — the investor outlay — then crosses zero at recovery (green marker). Redraws with every assumption change.

How recovery works

Investors fund Palonur's monthly cost base from launch, so the cumulative cash position starts negative and deepens while subscribers are few. As the base compounds, Palonur's recovery-phase share first covers the monthly cost (operational break-even), then begins paying down the accumulated outlay. "Investor recovery" above is the month the cumulative position crosses zero — every dollar investors put in has been returned. Beyond that point the business funds its own growth and the SLM year-end gift keeps compounding.

Sensitivity

Price and conversion are the dominant levers. Raising the AI usage tier or the annual plan, or improving conversion off the 120k base, pulls recovery forward sharply; higher churn pushes it out. Edit the assumptions on the Pricing page to test any scenario — these cards recompute live.
