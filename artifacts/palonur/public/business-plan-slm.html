Palonur Confidential · For Stanford Lifestyle Medicine
Partnership plan · Stanford Lifestyle Medicine newsletter & subscription

A trusted health newsletter for Stanford Lifestyle Medicine — built and run by Palonur, with the majority of value flowing back to Stanford.

Stanford Lifestyle Medicine (SLM) reaches roughly 120,000 visitors a month with no paid acquisition. This plan turns that audience into a recurring subscription — a curated newsletter plus a faculty-grounded AI agent that answers subscribers' lifestyle-health questions. Palonur builds and operates the product and carries the operating cost; Stanford keeps the brand, the faculty relationships, and the majority of the revenue. This version is written for SLM: it focuses on how costs and revenue-sharing develop over time. Every assumption is editable and every table recomputes.

What this partnership sets out to do
  • Turn SLM's existing audience into a subscription. Convert the ~120k monthly visitors into recurring members with a curated newsletter and a faculty-grounded AI agent — with no paid acquisition and no cost to SLM.
  • Keep the majority of value with Stanford. Palonur funds and recovers a lean operating cost; the revenue split is structured so SLM keeps the larger, growing share over time.
  • Give back as a year-end gift that builds an Institute. SLM's share is accrued and gifted annually. That gift funds a standing Stanford Lifestyle Medicine Institute — including a dedicated post-doc and operating support for Amy.
  • Credit and reimburse the faculty — and start a flywheel. The experts whose science powers the answers are recognised and reimbursed from a defined share of revenue. That sets a self-reinforcing loop in motion: more expert contributors make the content better, better content draws more subscribers, more subscribers grow revenue, and a larger reimbursement pool lets us pay contributors more — which attracts still more experts.

What SLM contributes

The brand, the faculty relationships, and an audience already arriving with lifestyle-health questions. SLM approves the science; Palonur never trains on faculty content and never publishes an answer that isn't grounded in approved sources.

What Palonur contributes

The product and the operating partner — all funded up front by Palonur, not by SLM. It is more than a newsletter: a faculty-grounded AI agent that busts the myths readers arrive with, gives an answer tailored to them and a concrete action to take, and points toward SLM courses for those who want to go deeper. Plus the newsletter tooling, billing, and the people who build and steward it.

How the money is shared

Subscription income is shared so that Stanford keeps the majority for the long term. Palonur takes a larger share only while it repays the cost base it funded, then steps down so SLM's share rises to 70%. The SLM portion is accrued through the year and distributed at year-end as a single gift — a partnership structured as a gift, not a vendor contract.

Where the gift goes

The year-end gift does more than reach Stanford's general fund. It is meant to stand up a Stanford Lifestyle Medicine Institute — funding a dedicated post-doc on the science side and operating support for Amy, so the work has a permanent home rather than depending on year-to-year goodwill.

Why the SLM share is a gift

The SLM portion is deliberately structured as a gift rather than a vendor payment. It is accrued through the year and distributed at year-end as a single contribution to Stanford Lifestyle Medicine. This keeps the relationship philanthropic and mission-aligned, and keeps the overwhelming majority of subscription value with Stanford.

It also seeds the longer-term goal: a standing Stanford Lifestyle Medicine Institute, funded by this gift — including a dedicated post-doc and operating support for Amy.

Faculty reimbursement, shown separately

Contributing faculty are reimbursed a defined share of gross subscription revenue, recognised separately from the Palonur / SLM split. It is shown for transparency throughout this plan and does not reduce the SLM gift or change the cost recovery math.

As the subscriber base compounds, the reimbursement pool grows alongside the gift — so the experts whose work powers the answers share directly in the product's success.

This is the engine of the partnership — a flywheel, not a one-off content cost. More expert contributors → better content → more subscribers → more revenue → a larger pool to reimburse contributors → more experts drawn in. Each turn of the loop funds the next, so the science deepens and the reimbursement pool grows together, year over year.

What Palonur funds up front

There is no marketing spend in the base case — distribution comes free through the SLM audience — so the operating cost is dominated by the people who build and steward the product: a post-doc on the science side and operating support for Amy, plus infrastructure. Palonur funds this flat, lean cost base from launch; SLM carries none of it.
