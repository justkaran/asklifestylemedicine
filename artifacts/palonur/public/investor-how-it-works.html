How a question becomes a signed Stanford answer.

Palonur is a verification pipeline, not a chatbot. A question routes through a Stanford-curated corpus, gets answered in a structured format, is queued for faculty approval, and ships with a cryptographic receipt that names the human behind it. The faculty corpus and the signed-answer ledger are the two assets other AIs cannot train their way around.

The pipeline — seven stages, one source of truth

1 · QuestionConsumer app or partner API call
→
2 · RetrievePillar-keyed vector search over Stanford corpus

3 · StructureLLM emits 5 labeled sections + refusal mode
→
4 · Faculty queueNamed signer reviews, edits, approves
5 · SignApproval writes signer + timestamp to ledger
→
6 · ServeCached, signed answer returned to user / partner
→
7 · ReceiptGraph receipt: Q → chunk → paper → signer → ts
→
Refuse pathIf no faculty-grounded answer: REFUSE / UNCOVERED
No prompt-only fallback. Every shipping answer has a human name attached or it is refused on the record.

How the faculty data is protected from training by other AI systems

Asset
Threat we block
How we block it
The corpus
Faculty papers, talks, clinical guidance, embeddings
Scraping bots, OpenAI/Anthropic crawlers, browser-tool agents copying raw chunks to seed a rival corpus.
Corpus never ships to the client. Embeddings live server-side; the API returns the synthesized answer, never the underlying chunks. robots.txt + noindex on every signed-answer URL. Rate-limited per partner key, no bulk export endpoint.
Signed answers
Q&A pairs with a faculty signature
Distillation — a competitor scrapes our outputs and fine-tunes a model to mimic "Stanford-style" answers.
Every signed answer carries an attribution string and a receipt id. License terms forbid use as training data. A distilled clone can copy the wording; it cannot copy "Zeitzer signed this on 2026-04-12" — that requires a relationship we own.
The refusal posture
When we say UNCOVERED
Competitors silently answer where we refuse, then claim broader coverage in demos.
Refusals are first-class outputs in the spec. Partners parse REFUSE / UNCOVERED as a feature, not a failure. Honest refusal at scale is what regulators, insurers, and legal teams require — copying it costs competitors their margin story.
The faculty queue
Steward workflow + edit history
A model that pretends to be reviewed without an actual reviewer.
Approval is a workflow tied to authenticated human accounts (Clerk + steward console + edit history). No public API can write to it. The provenance log is the audit trail regulators ask for — a prompt cannot fake it.
The /agent/spec contract
Machine-readable B2B endpoint
Partners using our spec to train a "Palonur-compatible" generic model.
Spec access is keyed, metered, and contractually scoped to inference-only use. Receipts let us prove which partner served which answer when. Misuse is detectable because the receipt graph is ours, not theirs.

Robust by construction

Structured outputFive labeled sections + two refusal modes. Malformed responses are rejected before reaching the queue.No silent confabulation can escape the parser.
Human-in-the-loopFaculty signer is the final gate. Edits write to history. Nothing ships unreviewed.Audit trail satisfies clinical and regulatory diligence.
Refusal as a featureIf the corpus doesn't cover it, we don't answer. Partners get a clean UNCOVERED they can route elsewhere.Failing loudly is what makes the trust claim credible.
Receipts as proofEvery answer carries a queryable graph receipt. Reproducible end-to-end.If it isn't in the receipt graph, it isn't ours.

Scalable by architecture

Pillar-keyed corpusSleep, exercise, nutrition, stress, social, substances, environment — each pillar is an isolated index with its own faculty lead.New pillars ship without rewriting the platform.
Warm-intro recruitingEach Stanford faculty member recruits the next pillar's lead. Then Stanford leads recruit global peers (Oxford, Karolinska, Mayo, Johns Hopkins, UCSF).Faculty acquisition cost falls per pillar, not rises.
One source of truth/api/sleep-agent (B2C) and /api/agent/spec (B2B) read the same signed-answer ledger.Same answer, same signer, same receipt — at any volume.
Cache the signatureApproved answers are cached by question-cluster + corpus version. New questions hit the queue; repeat questions are free.Faculty time scales as log of usage, not linear.

Defensibility comes from the relationships. Robustness comes from the refusal posture. Scalability comes from the cache. The corpus, the queue, and the receipt ledger are the three assets a competitor cannot scrape into existence.
